# Provenance Guard 

## Project Overview
Provenance Guard is a backend API that classifies submitted creative writing as likely AI-generated or likely human-written, scores confidence in that classification, surfaces a plain-language transparency label, and provides an appeals workflow for contested decisions.

---

## Detection Signals

### Signal 1: LLM Classifier (Groq — llama-3.3-70b-versatile)
**What it measures:** Semantic and stylistic coherence holistically — the overall
"feel" of the text including phrasing patterns, hedging language, vocabulary
choices, and narrative structure.

**Why it differs between human and AI writing:** AI-generated text tends toward
smooth, well-structured, evenly-paced prose with consistent register. Human
writing is more variable — it drifts, contradicts itself, uses idiom
inconsistently, and reflects a single person's voice rather than a statistical
average of many voices.

**Output format:** A float score between 0.0 and 1.0, where 1.0 = high confidence
AI-generated.

**Blind spot:** Black box — the model cannot explain why it scored a piece the way
it did. It can also be confidently wrong, especially on text that was written by
a human but heavily edited or polished.

---

### Signal 2: Stylometric Analyzer (pure Python heuristics)
**What it measures:** Four statistical properties of the text's structure:
- **Sentence length variance** — how much sentence lengths vary across the piece
- **Type-token ratio (TTR)** — vocabulary diversity (unique words / total words)
- **Punctuation density** — punctuation marks per 100 words
- **Average sentence complexity** — average words per sentence

**Why it differs between human and AI writing:** AI text tends to be statistically
uniform — consistent sentence lengths, moderate vocabulary diversity, predictable
punctuation patterns. Human writing is more variable and irregular.

**Output format:** Each metric produces a sub-score; they are averaged into a
single float between 0.0 and 1.0, where 1.0 = high confidence AI-generated
(low variance, low TTR, low punctuation density, high complexity uniformity).

**Blind spot:** Formal human writers — academics, legal writers, non-native
English speakers writing carefully — produce text that looks statistically
similar to AI output. This signal will over-flag polished human writing.

---

## Confidence Scoring and Uncertainty Representation

### Combining the two signals (Option B — Agreement-Sensitive Blending)
The two signals are combined based on whether they agree or disagree:

- **If signals agree** (|llm_score - stylo_score| ≤ 0.2):
  `confidence = 0.6 * llm_score + 0.4 * stylo_score`
- **If signals disagree** (|llm_score - stylo_score| > 0.2):
  `confidence = clamp(average(llm_score, stylo_score), 0.4, 0.6)`
  → disagreement is itself evidence of uncertainty; score is forced into the
  uncertain band regardless of individual values

### Rationale
LLM gets 60% weight because it captures semantic meaning that stylometrics
cannot. But when the two signals diverge significantly, neither should dominate
— the honest answer is "uncertain."

### Thresholds
| Confidence Range | Label Category        |
|------------------|-----------------------|
| 0.00 – 0.34      | Likely human-written  |
| 0.35 – 0.65      | Uncertain             |
| 0.66 – 1.00      | Likely AI-generated   |

### What a score of 0.6 means
A score of 0.6 sits at the top of the uncertain band. The system leans slightly
toward AI but does not have enough signal agreement to commit. The label will
reflect that ambiguity, not suppress it.

## Transparency Label Variants

All three variants must communicate clearly to a non-technical reader.

**High-confidence AI (confidence > 0.65):**
> "Our system found strong indicators that this content was AI-generated.
> This label is based on automated analysis and may not be perfect.
> If you are the creator and believe this is wrong, you can submit an appeal."

**Uncertain (confidence 0.35 – 0.65):**
> "Our system could not confidently determine whether this content was written
> by a human or generated by AI. It may be a mix of both, or the writing style
> may have unusual characteristics. If you are the creator, you can provide
> context through an appeal."

**High-confidence human (confidence < 0.35):**
> "Our system found strong indicators that this content was written by a human.
> Automated analysis is not perfect — if you have concerns, you can flag this
> content for review."

## Appeals Workflow

**Who can appeal:** Any creator who submitted the content (identified by
`creator_id`).

**What they provide:** `content_id` (from the original submission response) and
`creator_reasoning` (free text explaining why they believe the classification
is wrong).

**What the system does on appeal receipt:**
1. Looks up the original audit log entry by `content_id`
2. Updates the content status from `"classified"` to `"under_review"`
3. Appends a new audit log entry capturing the appeal timestamp, creator
   reasoning, and original classification
4. Returns a confirmation to the creator

**What a human reviewer would see:**
All entries with `"status": "under_review"` in the audit log, including the
original confidence score, both signal scores, and the creator's reasoning.
No automated re-classification is performed.

## Anticipated Edge Cases

**Edge case 1: Non-native English speaker writing formally**
A careful, formal essay written by a non-native English speaker will have low
sentence length variance and measured vocabulary — both properties the
stylometric signal associates with AI. The LLM signal may also score it
moderately high if the phrasing is unusually smooth. Both signals could agree
incorrectly → high confidence false positive. This is the most dangerous failure
mode on a writing platform.

**Edge case 2: Lightly edited AI output**
A user generates AI text and then edits it — adding personal anecdotes,
introducing typos, varying sentence length. The stylometric signal may score it
as human (higher variance after edits) while the LLM signal still detects AI
phrasing underneath. The two signals will disagree → score forced into uncertain
band → label says uncertain. This is the correct behavior: the system
acknowledges it doesn't know.

**Edge case 3: Very short submissions**
A haiku or a two-sentence micro-story gives both signals very little to work
with. Stylometric metrics become meaningless at low word counts (TTR is always
high for short text). The system should default toward uncertain for submissions
under ~50 words.

## Architecture

### Submission Flow

```
POST /submit  (text, creator_id)
      │
      ▼
 Assign content_id
      │
      ├─────────────────────────────────────┐
      ▼                                     ▼
[Signal 1: LLM Classifier]     [Signal 2: Stylometric Analyzer]
 llama-3.3-70b-versatile         sentence length variance
 → llm_score (0.0–1.0)           type-token ratio
                                  punctuation density
                                  sentence complexity
                                 → stylo_score (0.0–1.0)
      │                                     │
      └─────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                    ▼
        [Confidence Scoring Engine]
         if |llm - stylo| <= 0.2:
           score = 0.6*llm + 0.4*stylo
         else:
           score = clamp(avg, 0.4, 0.6)
                    │
                    ▼  confidence score (0.0–1.0)
        [Transparency Label Generator]
         < 0.35  → "Likely human-written"  label
         0.35–0.65 → "Uncertain"           label
         > 0.65  → "Likely AI-generated"   label
                    │
                    ▼  label text
           [Audit Log Writer]
            writes structured JSON entry:
            content_id, creator_id, timestamp,
            attribution, confidence,
            llm_score, stylo_score, status
                    │
                    ▼
         JSON response to client
         { content_id, attribution,
           confidence, label, status }
```

### Appeal Flow:

```
POST /appeal  (content_id, creator_reasoning)
      │
      ▼
[Look up entry by content_id in Audit Log]
      │
      ▼
[Update status → "under_review"]
      │
      ▼
[Append appeal entry to Audit Log]
 appeal_timestamp, creator_reasoning,
 original classification preserved
      │
      ▼
JSON response:
{ status: "appeal_received", content_id }
```

## API Surface

| Endpoint | Method | Accepts | Returns |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/submit` | POST | `text`, `creator_id` | `content_id`, `attribution`, `confidence`, `label`, `status` |
| `/appeal` | POST | `content_id`, `creator_reasoning` | `status`, `content_id` |
| `/log` | GET | — | array of structured audit log entries |
